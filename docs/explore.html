Our industry connections

UTSC Management’s co-op relationships give us a foundation for bringing industry further into the classroom. Its co-op model includes 12 months of paid work experience within a four-year undergraduate degree. Management co-op overview

MGOC15 with IBM Canada

The MAB materials describe Business Data Analytics as co-developed and co-taught by Andre Cire with IBM leaders Manav Gupta and Sadi Khan, including learning on campus and inside IBM Canada. The photographs include a session on retrieval-augmented generation evaluation and AI governance.

The course is an existing example of collaboration. The proposed certificate would explore whether that kind of engagement could extend across a coherent sequence of courses. Partner commitments to the certificate remain under discussion.

Reimagining recruitment

We could show prospective students what learning with industry would actually involve. A sample class or student demonstration can connect the recruitment message to a concrete course experience.

A practitioner-led sample class

At an applicant event, a practitioner and faculty member could introduce a business challenge and discuss how students would investigate it during a course.

Student work that applicants can inspect

With permission, show a project and explain how the student tested its results, responded to feedback and documented its limitations. This is an illustrative recruitment activity, not a claim of measured enrollment gains.

A visible path through the degree

Explain where AI learning, practitioner interaction and assessed project work would fit. Any pathway needs to account for prerequisites and the timing of full-time co-op work terms.

Co-op jobs remain subject to the normal application and recruitment process. Management student FAQ

AI certificate concept

A proposed 6–8 month pathway for Management students, with courses developed and taught alongside industry. The duration comes from the MAB concept notes. Academic requirements and participation are still under discussion.

The common foundation would cover business problem definition, data literacy, model evaluation and responsible AI. Students would need suitable technical preparation, with a preparation route for those with limited programming experience.

Illustrative course areas

The combinations below are examples for discussion: Google Agentic AI with Gemini, Microsoft agents and automation, and SAS analytics and decisions. Existing IBM participation in MGOC15 does not establish a commitment to this certificate. Google, Microsoft and SAS are prospective examples, not confirmed certificate partners.

GoogleAgentic AI with Gemini

Illustrative course area. Google participation in this certificate is proposed.

Tools

Google Gemini

Learning objective

Design an AI agent that breaks a business task into steps, uses tools and checks its results.

Example student assignment

Build a Gemini-based agent that gathers information from a supplied business dataset, compares options and drafts a recommendation. Test its tool choices and require human approval before any action.

Practitioner involvement

A practitioner could propose a business task and review the agent’s decisions and performance.

Learning across technologies

Compare how planning, tool use and evaluation carry across different agent platforms.

Official tool information: Gemini tool use and function calling

Illustrative course area. Microsoft participation in this certificate is proposed.

Learning across technologies

Students would compare approaches and explain their choices, reusing ideas across the course areas. A shared project would connect the courses to a business problem. It would not require combining every vendor’s products.

Faculty would determine academic learning outcomes and grades, with practitioner feedback contributing to student work. Assessment could consider problem formulation, testing, responsible use and communication to a business audience.

Decisions still to make

The credential name and issuing unit, credit status, prerequisites, number of courses, workload and timing need further design. So do faculty capacity, technology access and a sustainable commitment from partners. An academic certificate and a vendor certification would be separate credentials with their own requirements. Course participation would not imply a guaranteed job.

An illustrative student journey

Choose a business setting to see how the same academic structure could support different projects. These are educational scenarios, not descriptions of existing partner projects.

How could a service team reduce delays?

  1. Foundations

    Define the service problem, inspect the available data and choose measures of success.

  2. Partner courses

    Test a forecast, prototype an assistant for service requests, and compare the approaches against a simple baseline.

  3. Applied project

    Refine the solution after a practitioner design review. Test failure cases and decide where human review belongs.

  4. Demonstration of learning

    Present an analysis, a tested prototype and a business case that explains uses and limitations.

Advice from the board

Would this pathway help attract Management students and prepare graduates you would want to hire?

What capability would you most want students to demonstrate? Where could partners make the most useful contribution to teaching and feedback?
